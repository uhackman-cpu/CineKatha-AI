# Copyright and Source Rights Policy

**Status:** Initial project guidance, not legal advice.

CineKatha-AI should help users create responsibly. The application must not assume that a story, image, recording, font, or music track is free to use simply because it is old, online, or downloadable.

## User responsibilities

Users must have permission to submit and adapt source material, or have a defensible legal basis such as verified public-domain status or an applicable license. Users are responsible for checking the laws that apply to their intended publication and distribution.

## Literary works

Before treating a work as public domain, verify:
- The author's identity and date of death where relevant.
- The work's first publication and publication jurisdiction.
- Applicable term and transitional rules in the country of use.
- Whether the submitted text is a modern translation, adaptation, annotated edition, or edited version.
- Whether introductions, illustrations, cover art, notes, or recordings have separate rights.

A publication date before 2000 is not, by itself, proof of public-domain status. The status of an original work does not automatically clear a modern translation or adaptation.

## External media

For every sourced image, audio track, font, or video clip, store its origin URL, creator, license, attribution text, retrieval date, and any usage restrictions when available. Respect license conditions, including attribution, share-alike, non-commercial, and no-derivatives restrictions. Do not remove watermarks or bypass access controls.

Openverse and Wikimedia Commons can be useful discovery sources, but each asset's individual license and source record must be checked. Search-engine availability is not a license.

## Product behavior

The planned rights workflow should:
1. Ask users to identify the source and its origin.
2. Record the user's rights declaration and supporting evidence.
3. Mark uncertain cases as `review_required`, not cleared.
4. Keep provenance attached to derived scenes and media assets.
5. Generate an attribution/credits report.
6. Block or warn on assets with incompatible or missing rights metadata according to project policy.

Automated checks may assist research but must not claim to provide definitive legal clearance.

## Reporting

A future reporting process should allow rights holders to identify allegedly infringing material and request review or removal. Contact and response procedures will be documented before public production deployment.
